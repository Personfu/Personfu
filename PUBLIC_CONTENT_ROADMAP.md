# PersonFu / FLLC Public Content Roadmap

This roadmap turns the GitHub account into a public signal stream instead of a pile of disconnected repositories.

The goal is not to publish everything. The goal is to publish the right things: impressive, lawful, useful, source-backed material that makes people want to follow, star, revisit, and eventually convert into FLLC members or clients.

---

## Weekly cadence

| Day | Public drop | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | **OPEN-CLASSIFIED Report** | Public-source OSINT or defensive cybersecurity briefing with citations, summary, and action notes. |
| Wednesday | **Build Log** | FLLC platform, CyberCity/CyberWorld, SQL tooling, automation, or repository-control progress. |
| Friday | **Hardware / RF Bench Note** | Flipper, HackRF, SDR, telemetry, badgelife, or defensive signal-literacy writeup. |
| Weekend | **Engineering Log** | NASA ASCEND, simulation, data analysis, aviation/weather, or STEM evidence story. |

If life gets busy, the minimum viable cadence is one strong public drop per week. Consistency beats noise.

## Content lanes

### 1. OPEN-CLASSIFIED Reports

Public-source intelligence and defensive cybersecurity reports. The label is a public branding device, not a claim of government classification.

Good topics:

- CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities summaries
- NVD/CVE prioritization notes
- Public-sector source maps
- Cloud/security exposure checklists
- OSINT methodology notes
- Public infrastructure risk explainers
- Space weather / RF / aviation data source briefs

Output format:

- Executive summary
- Source list
- What changed
- Why it matters
- Defensive actions
- FLLC member upgrade path

Primary repository: [`Reports`](https://github.com/Personfu/Reports)

### 3. Internet Cartography Field Notes

Safe public education about how systems are organized: geography, network layers, logical routing concepts, public infrastructure records, and persona/context mapping.

Allowed public framing:

- Defensive inventory
- Asset management
- Incident response context
- Public-sector source validation
- Lab-only demonstrations
- High-level methodology

Avoid public operational abuse:

- No instructions for unauthorized access
- No credential collection
- No exploitation walkthroughs against real targets
- No stealth, evasion, persistence, or malware delivery guidance

## Repository policy

Every repository should eventually have one clear status:

- **Showcase for free** — polished public proof of skill.
- **FLLC paid content** — public preview, deeper member material.
- **Private/internal** — production code, raw sources, business logic, unfinished or sensitive work.
- **Archive/no action** — forks, references, stale experiments, or material that does not support the brand.
- **De-emphasize/control** — high-risk security references that should stay framed as defensive literacy or private governance inventory.

---

## Quality standard

Before a repo becomes public showcase material, it needs:

- A clear README
- A screenshot, diagram, or demo path when possible
- Install/run instructions if it is code
- Legal/ethical boundary language when it touches cybersecurity or RF
- No fake authority, fake classified claims, or overbroad government claims
- A clear tie-back to FLLC, PersonFu, or educational value
